Public Wi-Fi networks do not guarantee privacy. Some fake networks use names similar to those of real places, and a site's lock says nothing about the network used to reach it. Before connecting, confirm the name with someone who works at the place.

## Connecting to the right network

1. Ask the business for the exact network name and the password, if there is one.
2. On Android, open Settings → Network & internet → Wi-Fi and tap the name you checked.
3. Look at the signal strength in the list of networks. If the signal is weak, move to another place before entering any data.
4. If two networks with the same name appear, ask at the counter instead of testing both.

## Checking the site

When a page opens, check that the address starts with https and that the lock appears in the browser bar. This applies to login, banking, and shopping. The lock means the conversation between the browser and that site is protected; it does not prove the Wi-Fi network is trustworthy.

For bank accounts, saved passwords, or documents, use mobile data. Turn off Wi-Fi and let your plan's connection take over.

## Avoiding automatic reconnection

Android saves known networks and may go back to them on its own. After using public Wi-Fi:

1. Open Settings → Network & internet → Wi-Fi.
2. Tap the gear next to the network you used.
3. Tap "Forget" or "Remove" to delete the saved network.
4. To switch networks without leaving the screen, open the quick settings panel, tap the Wi-Fi control, and choose another network.

## If something goes wrong

- **You joined a network that was not the one at the place:** turn off Wi-Fi, turn on mobile data, and forget the network on Android. If you typed a password on a site, change it later on a secure connection.
- **The page opened without a lock:** do not type anything. Go back through mobile data and check that the address you typed is correct.
- **The phone keeps returning to the public network:** use "Forget" in the list of saved networks and check whether automatic reconnection is still on for other networks.
