WhatsApp is the most used app in Brazil — and that is why it is scammers' favorite entry point. There are three main scams: theft of your account, the "fake relative", and messages with malicious links. All of them depend on one thing: haste. This guide explains how each works and how to close these doors.

## Scam 1: account theft with the verification code

WhatsApp only installs your account on another phone if it receives a **6-digit code sent by SMS**. The scammer tries to get you to hand over that code:

- they call pretending to be support staff from a company, a bank, or even WhatsApp itself;
- they say your account will be blocked, or that you won a prize, or that you need to "confirm a registration";
- they ask you to "type the code that will arrive by SMS" — sometimes disguised as a survey or a promotion.

If you hand over the code, your account is installed on the scammer's phone. They start receiving your messages and asking your contacts for money.

**The 6-digit SMS code is yours and no one else's.** No legitimate support asks for that code. Never pass it on, never dictate it, never type it into a link you received.

### How to block it: two-step verification

Turn on your own PIN in WhatsApp (Settings → Account → Two-step verification). With it, even if the scammer gets the SMS code, they cannot install your account without the PIN. Full steps are in our two-step verification guide.

## Scam 2: the "fake relative"

Here your account is not stolen. The scammer creates a new account, puts up a photo of one of your relatives (copied from social media), and sends a message:

> "Hi Mom, I changed my number. This is the new one. I need to pay a bill today, can you send a Pix? I'll pay you back."

Variations: fake friend, fake company employee, fake customer service center.

**How to check:** call the person's old number, preferably by video. Ask a question only the real person would know. Be suspicious of any request for money by message, even from a known contact — friends' accounts get stolen too.

## Scam 3: malicious links and messages

Messages about an "unmissable promotion", "government aid", "job opening", or "a photo of you" with strange links. The goals: steal data on fake pages, install spy apps, or sign you up for paid services without you noticing.

Warning signs:

- unknown sender or international number;
- poor grammar and a sense of urgency;
- shortened link or a strange address (for example, `oferta-gov.xyz`);
- a request to install an app outside the official store (Google Play / App Store).

**Do not click, do not install, do not forward.** Delete and block the number. If it came from a known contact, warn the person through another channel — their account may have been stolen.

## Was your account stolen? Do this now

1. **Reinstall WhatsApp on your phone** and request a new SMS code. Once you sign in, the scammer is disconnected from their device.
2. If it asks for the two-step PIN you never set up (the scammer may have turned it on), wait for the recovery period shown by the app — and use your recovery email if you set one up.
3. **Tell your contacts** by other means (call, social networks, a status on another app) so nobody transfers money.
4. Turn on two-step verification as soon as you recover the account.
5. If money was requested from your contacts, file a police report.

## Quick protection checklist

- [ ] Two-step verification on with PIN and recovery email
- [ ] Profile photo and "last seen" visible only to contacts (reduces photo copying)
- [ ] Never pass on SMS codes
- [ ] Confirm any money request by phone call
- [ ] Do not click links in promotions received by message

Five minutes of setup now can prevent weeks of problems.
