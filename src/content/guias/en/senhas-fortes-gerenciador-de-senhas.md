"What is your password?" If the answer is the same for your email, your bank, and the online store, you have a serious problem — and it has a simple fix. This guide covers why repeated passwords are risky, how to create strong ones, and how a password manager stores everything for you.

## The problem is not just a weak password

When a site is breached (and this happens all the time), criminals take the list of emails and passwords and **try the same combination on other services**. This is called credential stuffing. If you reuse passwords, a leak on a small site takes down your email, your social accounts, and even your bank.

So the main rule is: **one different password for each service**. The obvious problem: nobody memorizes 40 passwords. That is where a password manager comes in.

## What a password manager is

It is an app that keeps all your passwords in an encrypted "vault". You remember **one master password** and the manager fills in the rest automatically, on your phone and your computer.

Reliable options to start with:

- **Google Password Manager** (built into Chrome and Android): free, already on your phone. A good starting point.
- **Apple Password Manager** (iCloud Keychain): built into iPhone and Safari.
- **Dedicated apps** such as Bitwarden (has a free plan) and 1Password (paid): more features, work on any browser and system.

Pick one and actually use it. The best manager is the one you really use.

## How to create the master password (the only one you memorize)

The easy method is a **passphrase**: put together 4 or 5 random words that form an absurd mental image — the stranger it is, the easier to remember:

> `cavalo-roxo-toca-piano-na-chuva`

Why it works:

- **Long** (more than 20 characters) — length beats complexity;
- **Easy to remember** — you picture the scene;
- **Hard to guess** — it is not a famous quote or a song lyric.

Avoid family names, dates, sports teams, `Senha123!`, keyboard sequences. And **never** reuse your master password anywhere else.

## Step by step: setting it up without mistakes

1. **Install the manager** and create the vault with your passphrase.
2. **Turn on two-step verification** in the manager itself (our two-step verification guide shows how). The vault is the most valuable target — protect it twice.
3. **Start with the critical services**: main email, bank, WhatsApp, social networks. Replace repeated passwords with passwords generated by the app (those giant random ones — you do not need to memorize them).
4. **Turn on autofill** on your phone and browser so the manager suggests passwords at login.
5. **Write the passphrase on paper** and keep it somewhere safe at home if you are afraid of forgetting it. Offline paper does not leak in bulk.
6. Gradually change the passwords on other sites. You do not need to do everything in one day.

## Habits that complete the protection

- **Never type your password into a link received by message.** Open the site by typing the address or through the official app.
- **Be suspicious of "confirm your password"** by email or SMS — banks and serious services do not ask for this.
- **Check whether your data has leaked**: the Have I Been Pwned site (haveibeenpwned.com) shows whether your email appeared in known breaches. If it did, change that service's password right away.
- **Do not write passwords in your phone's notes app** or in a WhatsApp chat with yourself. Those places are not encrypted like a vault.

## What if I forget the master password?

Most managers cannot recover it (that is how encryption protects you). So: paper kept at home plus a master password you use every day (it becomes familiar quickly). Some services offer a recovery code — keep it with the paper.

Start with your main email account. One unique, strong password there already blocks most attacks.
