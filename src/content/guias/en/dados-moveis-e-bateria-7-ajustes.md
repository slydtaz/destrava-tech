A data plan that runs out on the 20th and a battery that dies at 3 pm: two problems with several common causes. This guide gathers the settings that really make a difference on Android and iPhone — and clears up the myths that only waste your time.

## Part 1: making mobile data last

### 1. Find out who is using it

- **Android**: Settings → Network & internet → **Data usage** (or "Non-mobile internet"). See the ranking by app.
- **iPhone**: Settings → **Cellular** → scroll to the list of apps with each one's usage.

The usual culprits are: videos (YouTube, TikTok, Instagram), photo backup over mobile data, and automatic app updates.

### 2. Turn off automatic media download on WhatsApp

WhatsApp → Settings → **Storage and data**. Under "Media auto-download", turn off photos/videos/audio on **mobile data** (and review the Wi-Fi setting too). You will download only what you want to see — a big saving in busy groups.

### 3. Control photo backup

Google Photos and iCloud should back up **only over Wi-Fi**. Check each app's settings. A 4K video uploading over the mobile network devours gigabytes.

### 4. Turn on the data warning and limit

- **Android**: Settings → Data usage → **Data warning & limit**. Set your plan cycle and a warning (for example, 80% of your allowance). The system warns you before it runs out.
- **iPhone**: there is no native per-cycle limit; use your carrier's app to track usage and reset the statistics manually at the start of the cycle (Settings → Cellular → Reset Statistics).

### 5. Data saver mode and video quality

- Turn on your system's **data saver mode** (Android: "Data Saver"; iPhone: "Low Data Mode" under Cellular Data Options). Background apps stop wasting data.
- In video apps, choose lower quality when you are out (YouTube: quality settings for mobile data). The difference between 480p and 1080p is huge; on a phone screen, 480p looks fine.

## Part 2: making the battery last all day

### 6. The settings that help most

- **Screen brightness**: the screen uses the most power. Turn on **auto-brightness** and, if needed, lower it manually. OLED screens save a lot with **dark mode** (Android and iPhone).
- **Screen timeout**: reduce the time before the screen turns off by itself (30s–1min).
- **Background refresh**: turn it off for apps that do not need it (games, stores). Android: Settings → Apps → choose the app → Battery → "Restricted". iPhone: Settings → General → Background App Refresh.
- **Location**: leave it on only for apps that really use it (maps, transport). Android: Settings → Location → per-app permissions. iPhone: Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services. "Always" only for the essential ones.
- **Battery saver mode**: turn it on below 30–40% — or all day if you need it. It reduces background activity automatically.

### 7. Battery health (long term)

- Avoid leaving the phone **in heat** (car dashboard, direct sun) — heat really degrades the battery.
- You do not need to drain the battery: partial charges (20%→80%) are healthier than full cycles. Many phones have "optimized charging" — leave it on.
- A dubious third-party charger can damage the device. Use the original one or a certified brand.

## Myths you can drop

- ❌ **"Closing background apps saves battery"** — false. Android and iOS manage apps themselves; reopening an app from scratch uses MORE. Only force-close a frozen app.
- ❌ **"Miracle battery saver apps"** — most only show ads and kill processes the system restarts. Use the native modes.
- ❌ **"Charging overnight ruins the battery"** — modern devices stop charging at 100%. The real enemy is heat.
- ❌ **"5G uses more battery, so always turn it off"** — in an area with good 5G signal, consumption is comparable. If the signal is weak and keeps dropping, then locking to 4G helps (the radio struggles to find a network).

## Summary

Data: find the culprits, cut automatic downloads and backup over mobile data, turn on warnings. Battery: control the screen, restrict background activity, use saver mode, and keep away from heat. All of this is built into the system — no extra app needed.
