Two-step verification is the most important lock on your digital life — and it takes five minutes to set up. It works like this: besides the password (or the SMS code), your account asks for **a second factor that only you have**. Even if a scammer finds your password or receives your code by mistake, they cannot get in.

In this guide you will turn on protection for the two accounts that matter most in Brazil: WhatsApp and Google.

## Why this stops the most common scams

The WhatsApp account theft scam depends on a single weak point: the 6-digit SMS code. With two-step verification on, WhatsApp also asks for a **PIN you created**. The scammer may even get the SMS code — without the PIN, installing your account on their phone fails.

In your Google account, the second factor protects your Gmail, your photos, your documents, and your Android backup. Whoever gets into your Google account gets into almost everything.

## Part 1: WhatsApp (6-digit PIN)

> Menu names may vary slightly between app versions. The general path is the same.

1. Open WhatsApp and tap **Settings** (on Android, in the three-dot menu; on iPhone, in the bottom tab).
2. Tap **Account** → **Two-step verification** (in some versions, "Two-step confirmation").
3. Tap **Turn on** and create a **6-digit PIN**.
4. **Choose a PIN you can remember but that is not obvious**: no 123456, birth dates, or repeated digits. One technique: think of a 6-letter word and use the number pad (for example, "BRASIL" → 272745). Do not use the same sequence as your phone unlock code.
5. Add a **recovery email**. Do not skip this step. If you forget the PIN, the email is the only way to recover the account without waiting days.
6. Confirm. WhatsApp may ask for the PIN from time to time so you do not forget it — this is normal.

**Important:** the PIN is yours. WhatsApp never calls asking for it. If someone asks for your PIN by phone or message, it is a scam.

## Part 2: Google account

1. In your browser, go to **myaccount.google.com** and sign in.
2. Open **Security** in the menu.
3. Look for **Two-step verification** (or "How you sign in to Google") and tap **Turn on**.
4. Choose the second factor. Options, from most convenient to strongest:
   - **Google prompt**: a "Was that you?" notification appears on your phone that is already signed in. You tap "Yes". Simple and safe.
   - **Authenticator app** (Google Authenticator or similar): generates 6-digit codes that change every 30 seconds, even without internet.
   - **Physical security key** (USB/NFC): maximum protection, recommended for people who are frequent targets of attacks.
   - SMS/voice code: works, but it is the weakest method (scammers can clone SIM cards). Use it only as a backup.
5. Set up at least **one backup option** (for example, printed backup codes or a second phone). If you lose your phone, you need another way in.
6. Keep the **backup codes** in a safe place (paper stored at home, not on your phone).

## Part 3: what to do next

- **Turn it on for your main email too** if it is not Gmail (Outlook, Yahoo, etc. have a similar option).
- **Do not turn it off for convenience**: the small extra step at login is exactly what blocks the scammer.
- **Changed phones?** Set up the second factor on the new device before erasing the old one.
- **Forgot your WhatsApp PIN?** Use the "Forgot PIN?" option and your recovery email. Without a registered email, you may need to wait 7 days without using the account — which is why the email matters.

## In short

A password protects against guessing; two-step verification protects against theft. Turn it on for both accounts today.
