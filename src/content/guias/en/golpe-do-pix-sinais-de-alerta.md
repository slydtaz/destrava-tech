Pix is fast, free, and works 24 hours a day — that is exactly why it became scammers' favorite target in Brazil. Almost every scam follows a similar script, and you can recognize the signs before losing money. This guide covers the seven main warnings and what to do in each situation.

## Why Pix attracts so many scams

Unlike a bank slip or an old-fashioned transfer, Pix arrives immediately and, in most cases, **cannot be cancelled** once confirmed. Scammers know this: their whole strategy is to create haste so you transfer before thinking.

## The 7 warning signs

### 1. Manufactured urgency

"Now or never", "your account will be blocked in 10 minutes", "last chance". No serious institution demands immediate payment under threat by message. Urgency is the number one sign of a scam.

### 2. Request for a password, code, or token

No bank asks for a password, verification code, or token by phone, SMS, or WhatsApp. If someone asks, it is a scam — even if the person knows your name, CPF, and part of your data. Leaked data does not prove the person is from the bank.

### 3. A "fake relative" asking for money

Someone with a photo of your child, nephew, or friend says they "changed their number" and asks for an urgent Pix. Before transferring, **call the person's old number** (voice or video) and confirm. Scammers copy photos from public profiles.

### 4. Fake customer service center

You get a call or message from someone claiming to be a bank employee, reporting a "fraud attempt" and telling you to move money to a "safe account" or install an app. This does not exist: banks do not ask for a transfer to "validate security" and do not install apps over the phone.

### 5. Suspicious links by SMS, WhatsApp, or email

Messages with links to a "promotion", "refund", "CPF regularization", or "pending delivery" usually lead to fake pages that steal data or install viruses. Do not click. Go to the official site by typing the address yourself.

### 6. Government agencies asking for payment by message

Scammers pose as Receita Federal, Detran, and other agencies, offering "discounts" or collecting supposed debts via Pix. The Brazilian government publishes alerts about this scam. Debts and official services are checked on official sites and apps (gov.br), never through a link received in a message.

### 7. "Scheduled" or edited Pix receipt

In online sales, the buyer sends a receipt that looks real but is a scheduling (not a transfer) or an edited image. **A receipt is not payment.** Only hand over the product after checking the balance in your account, inside your bank's app.

## How to protect yourself

- **Set Pix value limits** in your bank's app (limit per transaction and night period). This greatly reduces losses if something goes wrong.
- **Register only keys you use** and be suspicious of anyone asking for your key "to deposit a prize".
- **Never make transfers guided by phone.** Hang up and call the bank's official number yourself (the one on the back of your card or in the app).
- **Turn on two-step verification** on WhatsApp and email — most scams start with a stolen account. We have a two-step verification guide for this.
- **Agree on a code word with your family** for real emergencies. If someone asks for money by message, ask for the word.

## Did you fall for a scam? Act quickly

1. **Contact your bank immediately** and ask for a precautionary freeze of the amount (the Central Bank's Special Return Mechanism, MED, can freeze the money in the scammer's account if there is a balance).
2. **File a police report** — it can be done online at your state's electronic police station.
3. **Keep all evidence**: screenshots, numbers, receipts, links.
4. **Tell your contacts** if your account or number was used, so nobody else falls for it.

Be suspicious, check through another channel, and do not act on impulse. A scam does not work against someone who stops and checks.
